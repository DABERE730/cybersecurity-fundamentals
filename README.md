# cybersecurity-fundamentals
Personal notes, concepts, and practical knowledge covering cybersecurity principles, threats, vulnerabilities, and security best practices.


## 1. Introduction to Cybersecurity

Cybersecurity involves protecting computer systems, networks, applications, and data from unauthorized access, attacks, damage, and theft.

## 2. CIA Triad

The CIA Triad is a fundamental cybersecurity model consisting of three principles:

- **Confidentiality:** Ensuring information is accessible only to authorized individuals.
- **Integrity:** Maintaining the accuracy and consistency of information.
- **Availability:** Ensuring systems and information remain accessible when needed.

## 3. Threats, Vulnerabilities and Risks

- **Threat:** A potential danger that could exploit a vulnerability.
- **Vulnerability:** A weakness in a system that could be exploited.
- **Risk:** The potential impact and likelihood of a threat exploiting a vulnerability.

## 4. Authentication vs Authorization

- **Authentication:** The process of verifying someone's identity.
- **Authorization:** Determines what an authenticated user is permitted to access or do.

## 5. Social Engineering

Social engineering involves manipulating individuals into revealing confidential information or performing actions that compromise security.

Common techniques include:
- Phishing
- Vishing
- Pretexting
- Baiting
- Tailgating

## 6. Phishing and Vishing

- **Phishing:** A fraudulent attempt to obtain sensitive information through deceptive emails, messages, or websites.
- **Vishing:** A form of phishing conducted through voice calls.

## 7. Common Attack Vectors

Attack vectors are the methods or pathways attackers use to gain unauthorized access to systems or information.

Examples include:
- Weak passwords
- Unpatched software
- Malicious attachments
- Compromised credentials
- Misconfigured networks
- Social engineering

## 8. Security Best Practices

- Use strong, unique passwords.
- Enable Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA).
- Regularly update software and operating systems.
- Avoid suspicious links and attachments.
- Apply the principle of least privilege.
- Maintain regular backups.
- Monitor systems for unusual activity.
- Secure network connections.

## Learning Objectives

To develop a practical understanding of cybersecurity principles, identify common security threats, understand vulnerabilities, and apply basic security practices.
